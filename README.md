# CS 255 – System Analysis and Design Portfolio

## DriverPass Project Reflection

### Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

The client for this project was DriverPass, a company looking to help students better prepare for their driving tests. They noticed that many students were failing their driving exams because they were not getting enough practice or proper training. DriverPass wanted a system that would allow students to take online practice tests, schedule driving lessons, track their progress, and manage their accounts. The system also needed to allow employees and administrators to manage appointments, customer information, and other business operations.

Throughout this project, I worked on creating a business requirements document and a system design document that explained what the client needed and how the system should function.

### What did you do particularly well?

I believe one of my strongest areas in this project was breaking down the client's requirements into smaller, more manageable parts. Instead of looking at the system as one big project, I focused on understanding what each user needed and how the different parts of the system would work together.

I also feel that I did well identifying the functional and nonfunctional requirements. Creating the UML diagrams helped me better understand how users would interact with the system and how information would move through it. Working in IT has taught me that understanding how something works is just as important as knowing how to fix it when something goes wrong.

### If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I could go back and improve one part of my project, it would be the UML diagrams in my system design document. Although I was able to show how the system should operate, I believe I could make the diagrams clearer by improving the layout, organizing the connections, and adding more details to certain processes.

One thing I learned is that creating a diagram is not just about connecting boxes and arrows. The information needs to make sense to someone who has never seen the system before. I would also spend more time reviewing the diagrams from the perspective of both the developers and the client to make sure everything is easy to understand.

### How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?

I started by reviewing the interview with the DriverPass representatives and identifying what they wanted the system to accomplish. From there, I separated their needs into functional and nonfunctional requirements. I used those requirements to help create the system design and UML diagrams that showed how customers, employees, and administrators would interact with the system.

I believe understanding the user's needs is one of the most important parts of designing any system. You can create the most advanced software in the world, but if it does not do what the customer needs, what is the point? A successful system should make things easier for the people using it, not create more problems. Listening to the client and asking the right questions early can save a lot of time, money, and frustration later.

### How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

My approach to designing software is to first understand the problem before trying to come up with a solution. I like breaking larger tasks into smaller steps because it makes the project easier to manage and helps me identify potential issues before they become bigger problems.

In the future, I would continue using techniques such as gathering requirements, creating UML diagrams, developing use cases, and reviewing the system from the user's perspective. I would also make sure to communicate regularly with the client and other team members throughout the development process.

One of the biggest lessons I learned from this course is that good software development starts long before anyone writes the first line of code. Planning, communication, and documentation are just as important as programming. After spending years working with technology, I have learned that it is always easier to prevent a problem than to spend hours trying to figure out why something is not working.

Overall, this course helped me better understand how systems are planned, designed, and developed. I can take what I learned from DriverPass and apply it to future projects and my career in IT.
